The digital representation

The environment is the foundation

Helios creates a faithful, navigable representation of the physical environment, giving teams a shared visual reference for understanding where things are, how spaces relate and what surrounds an asset or issue.

Point clouds, panoramic imagery, maps and spatial points of interest can form the navigable 3D environment commonly described as a digital twin. Helios extends that spatial foundation by connecting operational information and workflows directly to the places they describe.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions about Helios

Practical answers about integration, deployment and how Helios works alongside existing enterprise systems.

Helios is an enterprise spatial platform that connects the physical environment with operational information, enterprise systems and workflows. It gives people a shared spatial context for understanding and managing complex places.

Yes. Helios is designed to bring information from existing enterprise systems into spatial context. Integrations are shaped around an organisation's systems, information and workflows, so Helios does not require one specific EAM, IoT or content platform.

No. Helios can work alongside existing enterprise systems rather than replacing them. Where appropriate, those systems remain the authoritative source of information, while Helios makes their information easier to understand and use in physical context.

Yes. Helios is designed for desktop, tablet and mobile, with responsive interfaces and touch-friendly controls tailored to each device. The aim is to keep the core Helios experience consistent across devices, including on-site use, with only the changes needed for screen size, input method or device capability.

IBM Maximo is our most established EAM integration. Helios can expose Maximo assets, locations, work orders and related operational information spatially, while Maximo remains the system of record. Helios does not require Maximo. Learn more on the GraphQL for IBM Maximo page.

Sensor data can be brought into Helios from external sensor, building or source systems through configured integrations. Helios can then show current and historical readings against the relevant asset or location.

Yes. Helios can provide indoor routing with multiple stops, maps, written instructions and route preferences where navigation data supports them. Available routes depend on the configured environment and surveyed information.

Yes. Helios provides a shared spatial view for remote review and on-site workflows. Remote context can reduce unnecessary site visits when the available information is sufficient, but it does not replace site activity where physical attendance is required.
